Mountain biking in South Australia

Beginner trails near Adelaide

The easiest places to start mountain biking around Adelaide, and what the trail colours mean.

New to mountain biking, or just want a relaxed ride? These are the easiest places to start around Adelaide. For more parks, including the harder ones, see Where to ride.

The easiest places to start

  • Shepherds Hill Recreation Park (about 11 km south): easy family loops and a few short technical bits. A relaxed place for your very first trail ride. Directions
  • Belair National Park (about 25 minutes from the city): wide fire tracks that suit families and first-timers. You can get there by train. Directions
  • Cobbler Creek Recreation Park (northern suburbs): beginner loops, easy creek rides and small jump tracks that are great for kids. Directions
  • Kuitpo Forest (about 50 minutes south): marked loops and forest roads through the pines. Good for longer, easy rides. Directions

Ready for your first singletrack

Once you're comfortable on dirt, these parks have proper narrow trails (singletrack) with easy options:

  • Fox Creek Bike Park (Cudlee Creek, Adelaide Hills): SA's best-known trail park, with around 50 km of trails. Stick to the green trails at first. Directions
  • Sturt Gorge Recreation Park (southern suburbs): lots of flowy singletrack. It gets muddy after rain, so give it a day or two to dry out. Directions
  • O'Halloran Hill Recreation Park (about 16 km south): newer trails graded from beginner up, with climbing trails to get back up. Directions
  • Mount Crawford Forest (near the Barossa): a big network with family-friendly cross-country trails as well as harder runs. Directions

What the trail colours mean

Most Australian trail parks use the same signs. Start on white or green and move up when you feel ready.

  • White circle: very easy. Wide and smooth.
  • Green circle: easy. Gentle hills and a few small bumps. The best place for beginners.
  • Blue square: intermediate. Steeper, with roots, rocks and small drops.
  • Black diamond: difficult. Steep and technical. For experienced riders.
  • Double black diamond: extreme. Big features, for experts only.

Grades can be a bit different from park to park, so ride a new trail slowly the first time. The free Trailforks app shows the grade of every trail and any closures.

Tips for your first ride

  • Check your brakes and tyre pressure before you go, and always wear a helmet.
  • Bring water, a snack and your phone. Some parks have patchy reception, so tell someone where you're going.
  • Ride with a friend if you can, and walk any bit you're not sure about. Everyone does.
  • Give way to walkers, and let faster riders past when it's safe.
  • Many parks close on Total Fire Ban days, especially the forest ones. Check the CFS website in summer.
  • Stay off trails when they're wet and muddy. It damages them for everyone.
